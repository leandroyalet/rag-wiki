### 7. Contradictions (1)

#### C1 — Does RAG augmentation always improve generation quality?
- **Claim A**: RAG reduces hallucination by grounding outputs in retrieved evidence; augmentation improves performance across diverse knowledge tasks. → `concepts/Retrieval-Augmented Generation.md` [[lewis2020rag]], [[oche2025ragsurvey]]
- **Claim B**: For strong models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5 Sonnet) on well-documented domains, RAG augmentation *decreases* extraction coverage and introduces more hallucinations than base generation ("inverse calibration principle"). → `concepts/Hallucination in RAG.md` [[chatzikyriakidis2025raggedevents]]
- **Context**: Both can be true simultaneously — Claim A holds in low-resource/smaller-model settings; Claim B emerges in high-resource settings where parametric knowledge competes with retrieved context.
- **Tentative resolution**: RAG should always be evaluated against a no-RAG baseline; augmentation is not universally beneficial.
- **Status**: open
